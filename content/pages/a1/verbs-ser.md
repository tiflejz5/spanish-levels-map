# ser: identity, origin and profession

English has one verb "to be". Spanish has two, *ser* and *estar*, and they are not interchangeable. This page is about *ser*, the verb for what something **is**: a name, a nationality, a job, a lasting quality. You will meet *estar* (where something is, how someone feels) in unit 3. *Ser* is irregular, so learn its six forms by heart.

## How it works

| Person | ser | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| yo | **soy** | I am |
| tú | **eres** | you are |
| él, ella, usted | **es** | he / she is, you are (formal) |
| nosotros, nosotras | **somos** | we are |
| vosotros, vosotras (Spain) | **sois** | you are (plural) |
| ellos, ellas, ustedes | **son** | they are, you are (plural) |

- **Negative:** put *no* before the verb: *No soy de aquí.* (I'm not from here.)
- **Question:** the same words, with ¿ ? and rising intonation: *¿Eres de México?* (Are you from Mexico?)
- **Origin:** *ser* + *de* + place: *Soy de Chile.*
- **Jobs:** no *un* / *una* before the job: *Es médico.* (He is a doctor.)

## When to use it

1. **Name and identity.** *Soy Marta.* (I'm Marta.) *Es mi amigo.* (He is my friend.)
2. **Origin and nationality.** *Somos de Colombia.* (We're from Colombia.) *Es español.* (He is Spanish.) Nationalities are written with a small letter.
3. **Job.** *Soy profesor.* (I'm a teacher; a woman says *profesora*.) *Son estudiantes.* (They are students.)
4. **What someone or something is like** (a lasting quality). *Es inteligente.* (She is smart.) *Eres muy amable.* (You are very kind.)
5. **Day and date.** *Hoy es lunes.* (Today is Monday.)

## Examples

| Spanish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Soy Daniel. Soy de Canadá. | I'm Daniel. I'm from Canada. |
| ¿Eres estudiante? | Are you a student? |
| Mi madre es médica. | My mother is a doctor. |
| No somos de aquí. | We're not from here. |
| ¿De dónde es usted? | Where are you from? (formal) |
| Estos son Ana y Luis. Son de Madrid. | This is Ana and Luis. They're from Madrid. |
| Mi profesora es muy simpática. | My teacher is very nice. |
| ¿Sois hermanos? (Spain) | Are you brothers? / Are you brother and sister? |

## Good to know

- *Es* can mean "he is", "she is", "it is" and "you are" (formal). The context tells you which; add *él*, *ella* or *usted* only if it is not clear.
- With a job plus a description, the article comes back: *Es un médico muy bueno.* (He is a very good doctor.)
- For age Spanish uses *tener*, not *ser*: *Tengo 25 años.* See [[a1-verbs-tener]].

## Common mistakes

- ✗ *Soy un profesor.* → ✓ *Soy profesor.* — no *un* / *una* before a job on its own.
- ✗ *Soy 20 años.* → ✓ *Tengo 20 años.* — age is "having years" in Spanish.
- ✗ *Yo es de Chile.* → ✓ *Yo soy de Chile.* — each person has its own form: *yo soy*, *él es*.
- ✗ *Es en Madrid.* (where a person is) → ✓ *Está en Madrid.* — location of people and things takes *estar*; *ser de* is for origin (events are different; you will see them later).

## Practice

Complete with the right form of *ser*.

1. Yo ___ de Perú.
2. ¿Tú ___ profesora?
3. Nosotros ___ amigos.
4. Ellas ___ de Italia.
5. Usted ___ muy amable.

Translate into Spanish.

6. I'm not from here.
7. She is a nurse.
8. Where are you from? (to a friend)

## Answers

1. soy
2. eres
3. somos
4. son
5. es
6. No soy de aquí.
7. Es enfermera. / Ella es enfermera.
8. ¿De dónde eres? / ¿De dónde eres tú?
