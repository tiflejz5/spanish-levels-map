# Adjective agreement and position

An English adjective never changes: a white house, two white cars. A Spanish adjective follows its noun in two ways. It **agrees**: it takes the same gender and number as the noun. And it usually comes **after** the noun: *una casa blanca* is word for word "a house white".

## How it works

**Agreement.** Adjectives have up to four forms.

| Adjective ends in | masculine | feminine | masculine plural | feminine plural |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **-o** | alt**o** | alt**a** | alt**os** | alt**as** |
| **-e** | grand**e** | grand**e** | grand**es** | grand**es** |
| a consonant | azul | azul | azul**es** | azul**es** |
| nationality in a consonant | español | español**a** | español**es** | español**as** |

- *-e* and most consonant adjectives have one form for both genders: *un coche grande*, *una casa grande*; *un libro fácil*, *una pregunta fácil*.
- Nationalities in a consonant add *-a* for the feminine: *inglés* → *inglesa*, *alemán* → *alemana*. The accent disappears when a syllable is added.
- A **mixed group** takes the masculine plural: *Juan y Ana son altos.*

**Position.** Most adjectives go **after** the noun: *un coche rojo*, *una ciudad bonita*, *unos chicos simpáticos*.

These go **before** the noun:

- numbers and quantities: *dos libros*, *mucho tiempo*, *pocas personas*;
- *bueno* and *malo* often do, and they lose the *-o* before a masculine singular noun: *un buen amigo*, *un mal día*;
- *grande* before a singular noun becomes *gran* and means "great, important": *un gran hombre* (a great man), *un hombre grande* (a big man).

## When to use it

1. **Describing things.** *Es un coche rojo.* (It's a red car.)
2. **Describing people** with *ser*. *Mi hermana es alta. Mis amigos son simpáticos.*
3. **Saying where someone is from.** *Es mexicana.* (She is Mexican.) *Son españoles.* (They are Spanish.) Nationalities take a small letter.
4. **Giving an opinion with *muy*.** *La casa es muy bonita.* (The house is very pretty.) *Muy* never changes.

## Examples

| Spanish | English |
| --- | --- |
| una casa blanca | a white house |
| unos chicos simpáticos | some nice boys (or boys and girls) |
| Las calles son muy estrechas. | The streets are very narrow. |
| Mi profesora es colombiana. | My teacher is Colombian. |
| Es un libro fácil. | It's an easy book. |
| Los zapatos son azules. | The shoes are blue. |
| Es un buen restaurante. | It's a good restaurant. |
| Es un gran hombre. | He's a great man. |

## Good to know

- An adjective can also stand after *ser*, far from the noun, and it still agrees: *La sopa es deliciosa. Los tomates son caros.*
- Colors that are also nouns, like *naranja* (orange) and *rosa* (pink), usually do not change: *unas flores naranja*. Both *flores naranja* and *flores naranjas* are heard.

## Common mistakes

- ✗ *la casa blanco* → ✓ *la casa blanca* — the adjective takes the gender of the noun.
- ✗ *un rojo coche* → ✓ *un coche rojo* — colors and most adjectives come after the noun.
- ✗ *las chicas simpáticos* → ✓ *las chicas simpáticas* — gender and number both agree.
- ✗ *Ella es inglés.* → ✓ *Ella es inglesa.* — nationalities in a consonant have a feminine form.
- ✗ *un bueno amigo* → ✓ *un buen amigo* — *bueno* loses the *-o* before a masculine singular noun.

## Practice

Write the right form of the adjective.

1. una ciudad (bonito)
2. unos coches (rojo)
3. unas mesas (grande)
4. María es (español)

Put the words in order.

5. coche / un / azul
6. amigo / un / buen
7. simpáticas / las / chicas / son
8. Translate: It's a white house.

## Answers

1. una ciudad bonita
2. unos coches rojos
3. unas mesas grandes
4. María es española.
5. un coche azul
6. un buen amigo
7. Las chicas son simpáticas.
8. Es una casa blanca.
