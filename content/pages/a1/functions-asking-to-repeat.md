# Survival phrases: ask people to repeat, slow down, explain

Real Spanish is fast, and you will not understand everything. That is normal, and these phrases are your safety net. They let you keep talking instead of going silent. Most of them use *poder* (can): *¿Puede repetir?* (formal) or *¿Puedes repetir?* (informal). Learn both versions, and keep the formal one for strangers, shops and offices.

## Key phrases

**When you don't understand**

| Spanish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Perdón, no entiendo. | Sorry, I don't understand. |
| ¿Puede repetir, por favor? / ¿Puedes repetir? | Can you repeat that, please? |
| Más despacio, por favor. (also *más lento*, common in Latin America) | Slower, please. |
| ¿Puede hablar más despacio? / ¿Puedes hablar más despacio? | Can you speak more slowly? |
| ¿Cómo? / ¿Perdón? | Sorry? (I didn't hear) |
| No hablo mucho español. | I don't speak much Spanish. |
| ¿Habla inglés? / ¿Hablas inglés? | Do you speak English? |

**Asking about words**

| Spanish | English |
| --- | --- |
| ¿Cómo se dice "key" en español? | How do you say "key" in Spanish? |
| ¿Qué significa "llave"? | What does "llave" mean? |
| ¿Cómo se escribe? | How do you spell it? |
| ¿Cómo se pronuncia? | How do you pronounce it? |
| ¿Puede escribirlo, por favor? | Can you write it down, please? |

**Checking and thanking**

| Spanish | English |
| --- | --- |
| ¿Así? | Like this? |
| ¿Es correcto? | Is that right? |
| Ah, vale. (Spain) / Ah, okey. / Ah, ya entiendo. | Oh, OK. / Oh, I get it now. |
| Muchas gracias por su paciencia. | Thank you very much for your patience. |

## Dialogue

At a train station ticket office.

> **Clerk:** Buenos días. ¿Adónde va? (Good morning. Where are you going?)
> **Tom:** Perdón, no entiendo. ¿Puede repetir, por favor? (Sorry, I don't understand. Can you repeat that, please?)
> **Clerk:** Claro. ¿A qué ciudad va? (Of course. Which city are you going to?)
> **Tom:** A Toledo. ¿Cómo se dice "return ticket" en español? (To Toledo. How do you say "return ticket" in Spanish?)
> **Clerk:** Ida y vuelta. (Round trip.)
> **Tom:** Ah, vale. Un billete de ida y vuelta, por favor. (Oh, OK. A round-trip ticket, please.)
> **Clerk:** Son veinte euros con cuarenta. (That's twenty euros forty.)
> **Tom:** Más despacio, por favor. (Slower, please.)
> **Clerk:** Veinte… euros… con cuarenta. (Twenty… euros… forty.)
> **Tom:** Muchas gracias. (Thank you very much.)

## Culture note

- *Vale* is the Spain word for "OK"; in much of Latin America people say *está bien*, *bueno*, *ok* or *dale* (Argentina, Uruguay).
- A ticket for a train or a bus is *un billete* in Spain and *un boleto* in most of Latin America.
- People are usually happy to repeat. Showing you are trying is more important than speaking perfectly.

## Common mistakes

- ✗ *No comprendo nada, repite.* (to a stranger) → ✓ *Perdón, no entiendo. ¿Puede repetir?* — too direct with a stranger: use *usted* and a question.
- ✗ *¿Qué significa en español "key"?* → ✓ *¿Cómo se dice "key" en español?* — *significa* asks for the meaning of a word you heard.
- ✗ *¿Puede repetir, por favor.* → ✓ *¿Puede repetir, por favor?* — a question opens with ¿ and closes with ?.

## Practice

What do you say?

1. You don't understand.
2. Someone speaks too fast. (to a stranger)
3. You want to know how to say "bread" in Spanish.
4. You want to know what "llave" means.

Translate into Spanish.

5. Can you repeat that, please? (formal)
6. How do you spell it?
7. Can you write it down, please? (formal)
8. I don't speak much Spanish.

4. ¿Qué significa "llave"?
5. ¿Puede repetir, por favor?
