# Order in a café and buy things

In a café or a shop a few polite phrases do everything. Spanish speakers often order with a simple verb: *Un café, por favor* or *Ponme un café* (Spain, informal). The most polite way for a learner is *Quería…* (I'd like…), a past form used here as a soft request; you do not need to know the tense to use it. Waiters often use *usted*, and customers answer with *usted* too.

## Key phrases

**Ordering**

| Spanish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Quería un café con leche, por favor. | I'd like a coffee with milk, please. |
| Para mí, un té. / Yo, un agua. | For me, a tea. / I'll have a water. |
| ¿Me pone un bocadillo de queso? (Spain) | Can I have a cheese sandwich? |
| ¿Me da una empanada? | Can I have an empanada? |
| ¿Qué tiene de comer? | What do you have to eat? |
| ¿Tiene algo sin carne? | Do you have anything without meat? |
| Una botella de agua sin gas / con gas. | A bottle of still / sparkling water. |

**The waiter asks**

| Spanish | English |
| --- | --- |
| ¿Qué va a tomar? | What will you have? |
| ¿Algo más? | Anything else? |
| ¿Para tomar aquí o para llevar? | For here or to go? |

**Paying**

| Spanish | English |
| --- | --- |
| La cuenta, por favor. | The check, please. |
| ¿Cuánto es? | How much is it? |
| ¿Me cobra, por favor? | Can I pay, please? |
| ¿Puedo pagar con tarjeta? | Can I pay by card? |
| En efectivo. | In cash. |

**In a shop** (learn these as fixed phrases)

| Spanish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Estoy buscando una camiseta. | I'm looking for a T-shirt. (*playera* in Mexico, *remera* in Argentina) |
| ¿Cuánto cuesta esta? | How much is this one? |
| ¿La tiene en azul? | Do you have it in blue? |
| Me la llevo. | I'll take it. |
| Solo estoy mirando, gracias. | I'm just looking, thanks. |

## Dialogue

In a café in the morning.

> **Waiter:** Buenos días. ¿Qué va a tomar? (Good morning. What will you have?)
> **Laura:** Quería un café con leche y una tostada, por favor. (I'd like a coffee with milk and a piece of toast, please.)
> **Waiter:** ¿Con tomate o con mantequilla? (With tomato or with butter?)
> **Laura:** Con tomate. (With tomato.)
> **Waiter:** Muy bien. ¿Algo más? (Very well. Anything else?)
> **Laura:** No, nada más, gracias. (No, nothing else, thanks.)
> **Waiter:** Muy bien. (Very well.)
> **Laura:** La cuenta, por favor. ¿Puedo pagar con tarjeta? (The check, please. Can I pay by card?)
> **Waiter:** Sí, claro. Son tres euros con cincuenta. (Yes, of course. It's three euros fifty.)

## Culture note

- A *bocadillo* in Spain is a sandwich in a baguette; a *sándwich* is made with sliced bread. In Mexico a similar sandwich is a *torta*.
- In Spain you often pay at the end at the bar, and tips are small; in Mexico about 10–15% is usual.
- *Por favor* and *gracias* are used less often than in English in quick exchanges (especially in Spain); a friendly tone does the same job.

## Common mistakes

- ✗ *Quiero tener un café.* → ✓ *Quería un café.* / *Un café, por favor.* — you just name what you want.
- ✗ *Para yo, un café.* → ✓ *Para mí, un café.* — after *para*, *yo* becomes *mí*.
- ✗ *¿Puedo pagar por tarjeta?* → ✓ *¿Puedo pagar con tarjeta?* — you pay *con* a card, *en* cash.
- ✗ *Una agua, por favor.* → ✓ *Un agua, por favor.* — *agua* is feminine but takes *un* / *el*.

Write what you say.

1. You want a tea. (polite)
2. You want the check.
3. You ask if you can pay by card.
4. The waiter says *¿Algo más?* You don't want anything else.

5. —¿Qué va a ___? —Un café, por favor.
6. —¿Para tomar aquí o para ___? —Para tomar aquí.
