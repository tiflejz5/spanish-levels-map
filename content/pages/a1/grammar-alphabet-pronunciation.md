# Alphabet, pronunciation and stress

Spanish spelling is regular: once you know the letters, you can read any word aloud and usually write a word you hear. Compare English *though*, *through* and *tough*. Spanish has five clear vowels, a few letters that differ from English, and simple stress rules.

## How it works

**Vowels.** There are five, and each has one short, clear sound. They never change into "uh" as in English *banana*.

| Letter | Sounds like | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| a | *a* in *father* | *casa* (house) |
| e | *e* in *bed* | *mesa* (table) |
| i | *ee* in *see*, but short | *sí* (yes) |
| o | *o* in *go*, but short, without the *w* glide at the end | *ocho* (eight) |
| u | *oo* in *food*, short | *uno* (one) |

**Letters that differ from English.**

| Letter | How to say it | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **h** | silent | *hola*, *hotel* |
| **j**, and **g** before e / i | a breathy sound from the throat, like a strong *h* | *José*, *gente* |
| **ñ** | *ny* as in *canyon* | *España*, *mañana* |
| **ll**, **y** | *y* as in *yes* (in some regions closer to *j* in *jam*; in Argentina and Uruguay like *sh*) | *calle*, *yo* |
| **r** between vowels | one quick tap, like *tt* in American *better* | *pero* (but) |
| **rr**, and **r** at the start of a word or after n, l, s | a rolled *r* | *perro* (dog), *rojo*, *Enrique* |
| **b**, **v** | the same sound | *vaso*, *bar* |
| **c** before e / i, **z** | *th* in *think* in most of Spain; *s* in Latin America, the Canary Islands and parts of southern Spain | *cine*, *zona* |
| **c** before a / o / u, **g** before a / o / u | *k*; *g* in *go* | *casa*, *gracias* |
| **qu** | *k* (the *u* is silent) | *queso* |

**Stress.** Every word has one strong syllable. Three rules find it:

1. A word ending in a **vowel, n or s** is stressed on the **second-to-last** syllable: *CA-sa*, *HA-blan*, *LU-nes*.
2. A word ending in **any other consonant** is stressed on the **last** syllable: *ho-TEL*, *ciu-DAD*.
3. A **written accent** breaks the rules and shows the stressed syllable: *ca-FÉ*, *te-LÉ-fo-no*, *Jo-SÉ*.

**Questions and exclamations** have a mark at the start, upside down: *¿Cómo te llamas?* *¡Hola!*

## When to use it

1. **Reading a new word.** Say each letter, then put the stress where the rules say.
2. **Spelling your name.** People will ask *¿Cómo se escribe?* (How do you spell it?). The letter names: a, be, ce, de, e, efe, ge, hache, i, jota, ka, ele, eme, ene, eñe, o, pe, cu, erre, ese, te, u, uve (Spain) / ve (Latin America), uve doble (Spain) / doble ve or doble u (Latin America), equis, ye, zeta.
3. **Writing.** A missing accent or ñ can change the meaning: *papa* (potato, Latin America) and *papá* (dad); *ano* (anus) and *año* (year).

## Examples

| Spanish | English |
| --- | --- |
| hola | hello (the h is silent) |
| España | Spain |
| la calle | the street |
| el perro | the dog (rolled rr) |
| pero | but (one tap) |
| el teléfono | the telephone (te-LÉ-fo-no) |
| ¿Cómo te llamas? | What's your name? |

## Good to know

- *ch* sounds as in *church*: *chocolate*.

## Common mistakes

- ✗ *Como te llamas?* → ✓ *¿Cómo te llamas?* — a question needs ¿ at the start, and question words have an accent.
- ✗ *Espana* → ✓ *España* — n and ñ are two different letters.
- ✗ *pero* (for "dog") → ✓ *perro* — one r is a tap; rr is a roll, and it is a different word.
- ✗ *Jose* → ✓ *José* — the name is stressed on the last syllable, and the accent shows it.

## Practice

Where is the stress? Write the stressed syllable.

1. mesa
2. hotel
3. café
4. hablan

Write the word correctly.

5. Espana (Spain)
6. el pero (the dog)
7. Como te llamas? (What's your name?)
8. ola (hello)

## Answers

1. ME-sa (ends in a vowel)
2. ho-TEL (ends in l)
3. ca-FÉ (written accent)
4. HA-blan (ends in n)
5. España
6. el perro
7. ¿Cómo te llamas?
8. hola
