# Greet people, say goodbye and introduce yourself

These are the first sentences you will use, and you can learn them as fixed phrases before you know the grammar inside them. One difference from English matters from day one: Spanish has two ways to say "you". Use *tú* with friends, family, children and people your age; use *usted* with older people, strangers in formal situations, and customers or senior people at work. Many phrases below come in both versions.

## Key phrases

**Saying hello**

| Spanish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Hola. | Hi. / Hello. (any time, any person) |
| Buenos días. | Good morning. (until noon in Latin America; until lunch, about 2 p.m., in Spain) |
| Buenas tardes. | Good afternoon. / Good evening. (until it gets dark) |
| Buenas noches. | Good evening. / Good night. (after dark, also to say goodbye) |
| ¿Qué tal? | How's it going? (very common, friendly) |
| ¿Cómo estás? / ¿Cómo está usted? | How are you? (informal / formal) |
| Bien, gracias. ¿Y tú? / ¿Y usted? | Fine, thanks. And you? |

**Introducing yourself and others**

| Spanish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Me llamo Ana. | My name is Ana. |
| Soy Ana. | I'm Ana. |
| ¿Cómo te llamas? | What's your name? (informal) |
| ¿Cómo se llama usted? | What's your name? (formal) |
| Este es Pedro. / Esta es Lucía. | This is Pedro. / This is Lucía. |
| Mucho gusto. | Nice to meet you. |
| Encantado. / Encantada. | Pleased to meet you. (a man says *encantado*, a woman *encantada*) |
| Igualmente. | Likewise. |

**Saying goodbye**

| Spanish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Adiós. | Goodbye. |
| Hasta luego. | See you later. (also for "bye" in a shop) |
| Hasta mañana. | See you tomorrow. |
| Nos vemos. | See you. (informal) |
| Chao. | Bye. (informal, common in Latin America) |

## Dialogue

At a language exchange, two people meet.

> **Marta:** ¡Hola! Me llamo Marta. ¿Y tú? (Hi! My name is Marta. And you?)
> **Tom:** Hola, Marta. Soy Tom. (Hi, Marta. I'm Tom.)
> **Marta:** Mucho gusto, Tom. (Nice to meet you, Tom.)
> **Tom:** Igualmente. ¿Qué tal? (Likewise. How's it going?)
> **Marta:** Muy bien, gracias. Mira, esta es Elena. (Very well, thanks. Look, this is Elena.)
> **Elena:** Hola, Tom. Encantada. (Hi, Tom. Pleased to meet you.)
> **Tom:** Encantado, Elena. (Pleased to meet you, Elena.)
> **Marta:** Bueno, hasta luego. (OK, see you later.)
> **Tom and Elena:** ¡Hasta luego! (See you later!)

## Culture note

- In Spain friends and new acquaintances often greet with two kisses, one on each cheek; in much of Latin America one kiss is usual. Men usually shake hands with each other.
- *Buenas* on its own is a friendly short form of *buenos días* or *buenas tardes*.
- When in doubt, start with *usted*. People will tell you if they prefer *tú*: *Puedes tutearme* ("you can say *tú* to me").

## Common mistakes

- ✗ *Buenos tardes.* → ✓ *Buenas tardes.* — *tarde* and *noche* are feminine words, so it is *buenas*; only *días* takes *buenos*.
- ✗ *Me llamo es Ana.* → ✓ *Me llamo Ana.* — *me llamo* already means "my name is"; there is no extra verb.
- ✗ *Encantado* (said by a woman) → ✓ *Encantada* — the word agrees with the person speaking.
- ✗ *¿Cómo te llamas?* (to an older stranger at an office) → ✓ *¿Cómo se llama usted?* — use the formal version with people you would call "sir" or "madam".
- ✗ *Bien, y tú?* → ✓ *Bien, ¿y tú?* — a question inside a sentence still opens with ¿.

3. —Hola, me ___ Carlos. —Mucho gusto. Yo soy Rosa.
4. —Encantada. —___. (Likewise.)

6. What's your name? (to your teacher's grandmother)
