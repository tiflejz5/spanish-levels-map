# Family, describing people, nationalities, jobs

With *ser* and *tener* you can now talk about the people in your life: *Tengo una hermana. Es alta y muy simpática. Es médica.* Remember three rules from earlier pages: adjectives and many nouns change for women (*el médico*, *la médica*), nationalities take a small letter, and there is no *un* / *una* before a job on its own.

## Word partners

| Spanish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Tengo dos hermanos. | I have two brothers (or a brother and a sister). |
| Es hijo único. | He's an only child. |
| Mi hermana es alta y morena. | My sister is tall and dark-haired. |
| Mis padres son muy simpáticos. | My parents are very nice. |
| Mi abuelo es mayor y tiene noventa años. | My grandfather is old; he's ninety. |
| Es de Colombia, es colombiana. | She's from Colombia; she's Colombian. |
| Mi madre es profesora. | My mother is a teacher. |
| ¿Tienes novio? | Do you have a boyfriend? |
| Es muy divertido. | He's a lot of fun. |

## Good to know

- *El marido* / *la mujer* are used everywhere; *el esposo* / *la esposa* are also common, especially in Latin America.
- *Mayor* is the polite word for an old person. *Viejo* can sound rude about people.
- *Americano* can mean anyone from the Americas; *estadounidense* is only "from the US".

## Common mistakes

- ✗ *mis parientes* (meaning "my parents") → ✓ *mis padres* — *parientes* means "relatives".
- ✗ *Es Mexicana.* → ✓ *Es mexicana.* — nationalities take a small letter.
- ✗ *Mi hermana es alto.* → ✓ *Mi hermana es alta.* — the adjective agrees with *hermana*.
- ✗ *Ella es inglés.* → ✓ *Ella es inglesa.* — the feminine adds *-a*.

## Practice

Write the female form.

1. el abuelo
2. el profesor
3. Es español.
4. Es alemán.

Translate into Spanish.

5. my parents
6. I have a sister.
7. He's tall and nice.
8. She's a nurse.

## Answers

1. la abuela
2. la profesora
3. Es española.
4. Es alemana.
5. mis padres
6. Tengo una hermana.
7. Es alto y simpático.
8. Es enfermera.
