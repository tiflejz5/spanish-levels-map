# ser vs estar: the basics

You now know both verbs for "to be". This page puts them side by side, because choosing between them is one of the first real challenges in Spanish. The short version: **ser** says what someone or something **is** (identity, origin, job, character), and **estar** says **where** it is and **how** it is at the moment. The same adjective can go with both verbs and change the meaning: *Es aburrido* (he is boring), *Está aburrido* (he is bored).

## How it works

| ser | estar |
| --- | --- |
| identity, name: *Es Ana.* | location: *Ana está en Madrid.* |
| origin: *Es de Chile.* | feelings now: *Está contenta.* |
| job: *Es médica.* | states now: *Está enferma.* |
| character, looks: *Es simpática. Es alta.* | how it looks or tastes now: *La sopa está rica.* |
| time and dates: *Son las tres. Hoy es lunes.* | results of a change: *La puerta está abierta.* |
| where an event takes place: *La fiesta es en mi casa.* | *bien* / *mal*: *Estoy bien.* |

**Some adjectives change meaning:**

| with ser | with estar |
| --- | --- |
| *Es aburrido.* He is boring. | *Está aburrido.* He is bored. |
| *Es listo.* He is clever. | *Está listo.* He is ready. |
| *Es rico.* He is rich. | *Está rico.* (food) It tastes good. |
| *Es malo.* He is bad. | *Está malo.* He is sick (mainly Spain; everywhere: *está enfermo*). / (food) It tastes bad. |

## When to use it

1. **Ask "what is it like in general?"** → *ser*. *Mi ciudad es tranquila.* (My city is quiet.)
2. **Ask "how is it now?" or "where is it?"** → *estar*. *Hoy la ciudad está tranquila.* (The city is quiet today.) *La ciudad está en el norte.*
3. **Describing a person:** *ser* for who they are, *estar* for how they feel. *Pedro es tímido, pero hoy está contento.* (Pedro is shy, but today he's happy.)
4. **Talking about food:** *ser* for what it is (*La paella es un plato español*), *estar* for how it tastes now (*La paella está muy rica*).

## Examples

| Spanish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Soy de Colombia, pero estoy en Madrid. | I'm from Colombia, but I'm in Madrid. |
| Laura es médica. Hoy está cansada. | Laura is a doctor. Today she's tired. |
| La casa es grande y está en el centro. | The house is big and it's downtown. |
| Mi hermano es muy alegre. | My brother is a very cheerful person. |
| ¿Estás bien? —No, estoy un poco mal. | Are you OK? —No, I'm a bit unwell. |
| La película es aburrida. | The movie is boring. |
| Los niños están aburridos. | The children are bored. |
| ¿Estás listo? Son las ocho. | Are you ready? It's eight o'clock. |

## Good to know

- *Estar* + *muerto* (dead) is the normal form, although death is permanent: *El pez está muerto.*
- Some words take only one verb: *ser* + nouns (*Es profesor*), *estar* + *de acuerdo* (*Estoy de acuerdo*, I agree).
- A quick check: if you can add "right now" without changing the sense, *estar* is usually the answer.

## Common mistakes

- ✗ *Soy cansado.* → ✓ *Estoy cansado.* — feeling tired is a state now: *estar*.
- ✗ *Está médico.* → ✓ *Es médico.* — a job is what someone is: *ser*.
- ✗ *Madrid es en España.* → ✓ *Madrid está en España.* — location of places takes *estar*.
- ✗ *La fiesta está en mi casa.* → ✓ *La fiesta es en mi casa.* — where an event takes place uses *ser*.
- ✗ *Estoy aburrido* (meaning "I'm a boring person") → ✓ *Soy aburrido.* — with *estar* it means "I'm bored".
