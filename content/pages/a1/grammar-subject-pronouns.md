# Subject pronouns; tú vs usted

Subject pronouns are the words for "I", "you", "she" and so on. Spanish has more of them than English, for two reasons. First, "you" has a friendly form and a polite form, and a singular and a plural. Second, "we" and "they" show whether the group is all female. But Spanish uses them much less, because the verb ending already shows who is meant.

## How it works

| English | Spanish | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | **yo** | no capital letter |
| you (one friend) | **tú** | with an accent; *tu* without it means "your" |
| he / she | **él** / **ella** | *él* with an accent; *el* without it means "the" |
| you (one person, formal) | **usted** | often written *Ud.* |
| we | **nosotros** / **nosotras** | *nosotras*: only women |
| you (a group of friends) (Spain) | **vosotros** / **vosotras** | *vosotras*: only women |
| they | **ellos** / **ellas** | *ellas*: only women |
| you (a group) | **ustedes** | formal in Spain; all groups in Latin America. Often *Uds.* |

- A mixed group takes the masculine form: Ana and Pedro are *ellos*; Ana, Rosa and Pedro are still *ellos*.
- **Usted** and **ustedes** mean "you", but they take the same verb form as *él* and *ellos*: *¿Cómo está usted?* is "How are you?"
- There is **no word for "it"** as a subject: *Es grande.* (It is big.)

## When to use it

1. **Choose tú or usted.** *Tú* for friends, family, children and people your age in relaxed settings. *Usted* for older people, customers, officials and anyone you would call "sir" or "madam". To a friend: *¿Y tú?* To your new boss: *¿Y usted?*
2. **Talk to a group.** In Spain, *vosotros* for friends and *ustedes* for formal groups. In Latin America, *ustedes* for every group. Both are correct; use the one people use where you are.
3. **Leave the pronoun out.** In a normal sentence the verb is enough: *Me llamo Ana* (not *Yo me llamo Ana* every time).
4. **Use the pronoun for contrast or emphasis.** *Yo me llamo Ana y él se llama Pedro.* (I'm Ana, and **he** is Pedro.) *—¿Quién es Marta? —Yo.* (Me.)

## Examples

| Spanish | English |
| --- | --- |
| —Bien, gracias. ¿Y tú? | Fine, thanks. And you? |
| —Muy bien. ¿Y usted, señora? | Very well. And you, madam? |
| Él se llama Luis y ella se llama Rosa. | He's called Luis and she's called Rosa. |
| ¿Cómo se llama usted? | What's your name? (formal) |
| —¿Y vosotros? —Nosotros, bien. (Spain) | "And you guys?" "We're fine." |
| —¿Y ustedes? —Nosotras, muy bien. | "And you?" "We're very well." (a group of women) |
| Mucho gusto. Yo soy Pablo. | Nice to meet you. I'm Pablo. |

## Good to know

- In Argentina, Uruguay, Paraguay and much of Central America (and in parts of other countries) people say *vos* instead of *tú* with different verb forms (*vos sos*, *vos tenés*). You will understand it from context; this course uses *tú*.

## Common mistakes

- ✗ *Yo me llamo Ana. Yo soy de Lima. Yo soy profesora.* → ✓ *Me llamo Ana. Soy de Lima. Soy profesora.* — repeating *yo* in every sentence sounds unnatural.
- ✗ *Usted te llamas Pedro.* → ✓ *Usted se llama Pedro.* — *usted* takes the forms of *él*, not of *tú*.
- ✗ *Ellos* (for Ana and María) → ✓ *Ellas* — a group of only women is *ellas*, and *nosotras* for "we".
- ✗ *Él es grande.* (about a table) → ✓ *Es grande.* — there is no subject pronoun for "it".
- ✗ *tu* (for "you") → ✓ *tú* — without the accent, *tu* means "your".

## Practice

Which pronoun? Write it in Spanish.

1. you, to your best friend
2. you, to an old man you meet in a museum
3. they (Elena and Sofía)
4. we (you and your brother; you are a woman)

Translate into Spanish.

5. And you? (to a group of friends in Mexico)
6. And you? (to a group of friends in Madrid)
7. He's called Pablo.
8. What's your name? (to a hotel receptionist)

## Answers

1. tú
2. usted
3. ellas
4. nosotros
5. ¿Y ustedes?
6. ¿Y vosotros? / ¿Y vosotras? (¿Y ustedes? is also understood)
7. Se llama Pablo. / Él se llama Pablo.
8. ¿Cómo se llama usted? / ¿Cómo se llama?
