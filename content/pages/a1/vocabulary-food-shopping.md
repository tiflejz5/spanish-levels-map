# Food, drink, shops and prices

Food is the easiest place to practice: you buy it every day. Learn each noun with its article, and notice two habits. Spanish talks about food in general with the article (*Me gusta el pan*), but asks for an amount without it (*Quiero pan*). And prices use *costar* (o → ue): *¿Cuánto cuesta?* for one thing, *¿Cuánto cuestan?* for several.

## Good to know

- *El pescado* is fish as food; a live fish is *el pez*.
- Many prices are said with *con*: *2,50 €* is *dos euros con cincuenta* (or *dos cincuenta*). Spain and much of Latin America write a decimal comma: *2,50*; Mexico and some other countries use a decimal point: *2.50*.
- *Papa* (potato) is Latin American; in Spain it is *patata*. *Jugo* is the word for juice in Latin America; *zumo* is the word in Spain.

## Common mistakes

- ✗ *¿Cuánto cuesta los tomates?* → ✓ *¿Cuánto cuestan los tomates?* — the verb agrees with the plural noun.
- ✗ *Me gusta café.* → ✓ *Me gusta el café.* — food in general takes the article after *gustar*.
- ✗ *un kilo tomates* → ✓ *un kilo de tomates* — amounts need *de*.
- ✗ *la agua* → ✓ *el agua* — *agua* is feminine but takes *el* in the singular.

## Practice

Write the word with the article.

1. bread
2. cheese
3. water
4. chicken

Translate into Spanish.

5. How much are the oranges?
6. A kilo of apples, please.
7. I don't eat fish.
8. A bottle of milk.

## Answers

1. el pan
2. el queso
3. el agua
4. el pollo
5. ¿Cuánto cuestan las naranjas?
6. Un kilo de manzanas, por favor.
7. No como pescado.
8. Una botella de leche.
