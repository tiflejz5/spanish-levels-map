# Reflexive verbs for the daily routine

Many everyday actions are **reflexive** in Spanish: you do them to yourself. English often says "I get up", "I shower", "I go to bed" with no extra word; Spanish adds a small pronoun: *me levanto*, *me ducho*, *me acuesto*. You already know one reflexive verb: *llamarse* (*me llamo Ana*). In the dictionary these verbs end in **-se**: *levantarse*, *ducharse*.

## How it works

The pronoun (*me, te, se, nos, os, se*) goes **before** the conjugated verb.

| Person | levantarse (to get up) | ducharse (to shower) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| yo | **me** levanto | **me** ducho |
| tú | **te** levantas | **te** duchas |
| él, ella, usted | **se** levanta | **se** ducha |
| nosotros, nosotras | **nos** levantamos | **nos** duchamos |
| vosotros, vosotras (Spain) | **os** levantáis | **os** ducháis |
| ellos, ellas, ustedes | **se** levantan | **se** duchan |

- **Negative:** *no* before the pronoun: *No me levanto temprano.*
- **Question:** *¿A qué hora te levantas?* (What time do you get up?)
- Some are also stem-changing: *despertarse* (e → ie): *me despierto*; *acostarse* (o → ue): *me acuesto*; *vestirse* (e → i): *me visto*.

**Routine verbs:** *despertarse* (to wake up), *levantarse* (to get up), *ducharse* (to shower), *lavarse* (to wash; *lavarse los dientes* = to brush your teeth, also *cepillarse los dientes*), *vestirse* (to get dressed), *peinarse* (to comb your hair), *acostarse* (to go to bed), *dormirse* (to fall asleep).

**Not reflexive, but part of the day:** *desayunar* (to have breakfast), *comer* (to eat; in Spain and Mexico also "to have lunch"; many Latin American countries say *almorzar*), *cenar* (to have dinner), *trabajar*, *salir de casa*, *volver a casa*.

## When to use it

1. **Describing your day.** *Me levanto a las siete y me ducho.* (I get up at seven and shower.)
2. **Asking about someone's routine.** *¿A qué hora te acuestas?* (What time do you go to bed?)
3. **Saying what you do to your body.** *Me lavo las manos.* (I wash my hands.) Spanish uses *las*, not *mis*: the pronoun already shows whose hands.

## Examples

| Spanish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Me despierto a las seis y media. | I wake up at six thirty. |
| ¿Te duchas por la mañana o por la noche? | Do you shower in the morning or at night? |
| Mi hijo se viste solo. | My son gets dressed on his own. |
| Nos acostamos tarde los sábados. | We go to bed late on Saturdays. |
| Desayuno café con pan tostado. | I have coffee and toast for breakfast. |
| Mis padres se levantan muy temprano. | My parents get up very early. |
| No me peino nunca. | I never comb my hair. |
| ¿A qué hora os levantáis? (Spain) | What time do you (all) get up? |

## Good to know

- With an infinitive, the pronoun can go at the end and joins the verb: *Quiero ducharme.* = *Me quiero duchar.* (I want to take a shower.)
- Lunch is the main meal in Spain and much of Latin America; in Spain it is often around 2 p.m.
- More on times in [[a1-functions-time-dates]].

## Common mistakes

- ✗ *Levanto a las siete.* → ✓ *Me levanto a las siete.* — "get up" needs the reflexive pronoun.
- ✗ *Me lavo mis dientes.* → ✓ *Me lavo los dientes.* — with body parts Spanish uses *los / las*, not *mis*.
- ✗ *Ella me ducha.* (meaning "she showers") → ✓ *Ella se ducha.* — the pronoun matches the person: *se* for *ella*.
- ✗ *Me acosto a las once.* → ✓ *Me acuesto a las once.* — *acostarse* changes o to ue.

## Practice

Complete with the pronoun and the verb.

1. Yo ___ ___ (levantarse) a las siete.
2. ¿Tú ___ ___ (ducharse) por la mañana?
3. Ana ___ ___ (acostarse) tarde.
4. Nosotros ___ ___ (despertarse) temprano.

Translate into Spanish.

5. I get dressed.
6. What time do you go to bed? (to a friend)
7. My parents get up at six.
8. I brush my teeth.

## Answers

1. me levanto
2. te duchas
3. se acuesta
4. nos despertamos
5. Me visto.
6. ¿A qué hora te acuestas?
7. Mis padres se levantan a las seis.
8. Me lavo los dientes.
