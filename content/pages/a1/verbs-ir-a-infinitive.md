# ir a + infinitive: plans

You already know *ir* (to go): *voy, vas, va, vamos, vais, van*. Put *a* and an infinitive after it and you have the easiest way to talk about the future: *Voy a estudiar* (I'm going to study). It works just like English "going to", so it is the first future form most learners use. *Vamos a* + infinitive can also mean "let's": *Vamos a ver.* (Let's see.)

## How it works

**ir (present) + a + infinitive**

| Person | ir a + infinitive | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| yo | **voy a** comer | I'm going to eat |
| tú | **vas a** comer | you're going to eat |
| él, ella, usted | **va a** comer | he / she is going to eat, you're going to eat |
| nosotros, nosotras | **vamos a** comer | we're going to eat |
| vosotros, vosotras (Spain) | **vais a** comer | you're going to eat |
| ellos, ellas, ustedes | **van a** comer | they're going to eat, you're going to eat |

- Only *ir* changes; *a* and the infinitive stay the same.
- **Negative:** *No voy a salir.* (I'm not going to go out.)
- **Question:** *¿Qué vas a hacer mañana?* (What are you going to do tomorrow?)
- **Reflexive verbs:** the pronoun goes before *ir* or joins the infinitive: *Me voy a acostar.* = *Voy a acostarme.*

**Time words for the future:** *mañana* (tomorrow), *pasado mañana* (the day after tomorrow), *esta tarde* (this afternoon), *esta noche* (tonight), *este fin de semana* (this weekend), *la semana que viene* / *la próxima semana* (next week), *el año que viene* (next year).

## When to use it

1. **Plans.** *Este verano voy a viajar a México.* (This summer I'm going to travel to Mexico.)
2. **Intentions.** *Voy a estudiar más.* (I'm going to study more.)
3. **Something you can see is about to happen.** *¡Cuidado! Te vas a caer.* (Careful! You're going to fall.)
4. **Suggestions with *vamos a*.** *Vamos a empezar.* (Let's start.)
5. **Asking about plans.** *¿Qué vais a hacer el sábado?* (Spain) / *¿Qué van a hacer el sábado?*

## Examples

| Spanish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Voy a visitar a mi madre. | I'm going to visit my mother. |
| ¿Qué vas a hacer esta noche? | What are you going to do tonight? |
| Mañana vamos a comer en un restaurante. | Tomorrow we're going to eat in a restaurant. |
| Mis amigos van a venir a las ocho. | My friends are going to come at eight. |
| No voy a trabajar el lunes. | I'm not going to work on Monday. |
| Va a llover. | It's going to rain. |
| Vamos a ver. | Let's see. |
| ¿Vas a venir a la fiesta? | Are you going to come to the party? |

## Good to know

- When the object of the verb is a person, add *a*: *Voy a visitar **a** mi madre*, *llamar a Ana* (but *visitar Roma*). This "personal a" has no English equivalent.
- *Voy a la playa* (I'm going to the beach) is *ir a* + a place, not a plan with an infinitive. Both use *a*.
- The present can also talk about fixed plans: *Mañana trabajo.* *Ir a* puts the intention first. See the card *ir a + infinitivo* in the Tense & Mood Lab.

## Common mistakes

- ✗ *Voy comer.* → ✓ *Voy a comer.* — do not leave out *a*.
- ✗ *Vamos a salimos.* → ✓ *Vamos a salir.* — after *a* comes the infinitive.
- ✗ *Yo va a estudiar.* → ✓ *Yo voy a estudiar.* — *ir* changes with the person.
- ✗ *Voy a visitar mi madre.* → ✓ *Voy a visitar a mi madre.* — a person after *visitar* takes *a*.

2. vas a venir
