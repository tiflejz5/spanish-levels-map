# estar for location and states; hay vs está

*Estar* is the second Spanish verb for "to be". You use it for **where** a person or thing is (*Estoy en casa*) and for **how** someone or something is at the moment (*Estoy cansado*). Next to it stands *hay*, "there is / there are", which says that something **exists** somewhere: *Hay un banco en la plaza.* English uses "to be" for all of this, so these three are worth learning together.

## How it works

| Person | estar | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| yo | **estoy** | I am |
| tú | **estás** | you are |
| él, ella, usted | **está** | he / she is, you are (formal) |
| nosotros, nosotras | **estamos** | we are |
| vosotros, vosotras (Spain) | **estáis** | you are (plural) |
| ellos, ellas, ustedes | **están** | they are, you are (plural) |

- Watch the accents: *estás*, *está*, *estáis*, *están*. *Esta* without an accent means "this".
- **Location:** *estar* + *en* / *aquí* / *allí* …: *Estamos en el hotel.*
- **States and feelings:** *estar* + adjective, which agrees: *Ana está cansada. Los niños están contentos.*

**Hay** (from the verb *haber*) has one form for singular and plural:

- *Hay* + **a / an, numbers, or no article**: *Hay un parque. Hay dos bancos. Hay pan.*
- *Está / están* + **the, a name or my**: *El parque está allí. Mis llaves están en la mesa.*

Ask with *¿Hay…?* when you don't know if something exists, and with *¿Dónde está…?* when you know it does: *¿Hay un banco por aquí?* (Is there a bank around here?) *¿Dónde está el banco?* (Where is the bank?)

## When to use it

1. **Where someone or something is.** *¿Dónde estás? —Estoy en el trabajo.* (I'm at work.)
2. **How someone feels now.** *Estoy cansado.* (I'm tired.) *¿Estás bien?* (Are you OK?)
3. **The state of something.** *La puerta está abierta.* (The door is open.) *El café está frío.* (The coffee is cold.)
4. **Asking and saying how someone is.** *¿Cómo estás? —Muy bien, gracias.*
5. **Saying what there is in a place:** *hay*. *En mi calle hay dos restaurantes.* (There are two restaurants on my street.)

## Examples

| Spanish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Estoy en casa. | I'm at home. |
| ¿Dónde están las llaves? | Where are the keys? |
| Marta está enferma hoy. | Marta is sick today. |
| Estamos muy contentos. | We're very happy. |
| La tienda está cerrada. | The store is closed. |
| Hay un supermercado en la esquina. | There's a supermarket on the corner. |
| ¿Hay wifi en el hotel? | Is there wifi in the hotel? |
| No hay leche. | There's no milk. |

## Good to know

- *Hay* has no plural form: *Hay una farmacia. Hay tres farmacias.* Never *hayn*.
- *Estar* with *bien* / *mal*: *Estoy bien.* (I'm fine.) *Bien* never changes.
- The difference between *ser* and *estar* with adjectives (*es aburrido* / *está aburrido*) comes in [[a1-verbs-ser-vs-estar-basics]].

## Common mistakes

- ✗ *Soy en casa.* → ✓ *Estoy en casa.* — where you are takes *estar*.
- ✗ *Hay el banco en la plaza.* → ✓ *El banco está en la plaza.* — with *el*, *la* use *está*; *hay* is for something new (*hay un banco*).
- ✗ *Están dos farmacias en mi calle.* → ✓ *Hay dos farmacias en mi calle.* — "there are" is *hay*.
- ✗ *Ella esta cansada.* → ✓ *Ella está cansada.* — *esta* without the accent means "this".
- ✗ *Estoy cansado* (said by a woman) → ✓ *Estoy cansada.* — the adjective agrees with the speaker.
