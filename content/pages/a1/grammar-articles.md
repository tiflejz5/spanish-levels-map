# Definite and indefinite articles; al and del

Spanish articles work like English "the" and "a", with two differences. They change with the **gender** and **number** of the noun, so there are four of each. And Spanish uses "the" more often than English: for things in general (*la vida*, life), with titles (*el señor García*) and with days (*el lunes*). If your language has no articles at all, this is the topic to practice most.

## How it works

| | masculine | feminine |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **the** (singular) | **el** libro | **la** mesa |
| **the** (plural) | **los** libros | **las** mesas |
| **a / an** | **un** libro | **una** mesa |
| **some** | **unos** libros | **unas** mesas |

**Two short forms.** *A* (to) and *de* (of, from) join with *el*:

- *a* + *el* = **al**: *el cine* → *Voy al cine.* (I'm going to the movies.)
- *de* + *el* = **del**: *el profesor* → *el libro del profesor* (the teacher's book)
- Only with *el*: *a la*, *de la*, *a los*, *de las* do not change.

**A feminine noun that starts with a stressed a- takes *el* and *un*** in the singular, to avoid two *a* sounds: *el agua* (water), *un aula* (a classroom). It is still feminine: *el agua fría*, *las aguas*.

## When to use it

1. **Something specific, or already mentioned:** *el*, *la*, *los*, *las*. *Es la casa de Ana.* (It's Ana's house.)
2. **Something new, one of many:** *un*, *una*. *Es un problema.* (It's a problem.)
3. **Things in general** take *el / la / los / las*, where English uses no article: *El café es caro aquí.* (Coffee is expensive here.) *Los niños son curiosos.* (Children are curious.)
4. **Talking about a person with a title:** *el señor García*, *la doctora Ruiz*. But not when you speak **to** the person: *Buenos días, señor García.*
5. **Days of the week:** *el lunes* (on Monday), *los lunes* (on Mondays).
6. **No article** with a job after *ser* (*Es médico*), with most countries (*Soy de Perú*), and for an unspecified amount of something: *Hay pan y leche.* (There's bread and milk.)

## Examples

| Spanish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Es un libro. Es el libro de Marta. | It's a book. It's Marta's book. |
| Las mesas son nuevas. | The tables are new. |
| El café es caro aquí. | Coffee is expensive here. |
| El señor García es de Chile. | Mr. García is from Chile. |
| Buenos días, señora Ruiz. | Good morning, Mrs. Ruiz. |
| Voy al cine el sábado. | I'm going to the movies on Saturday. |
| Es el coche del profesor. | It's the teacher's car. |
| Son unos amigos de México. | They're friends from Mexico. |

## Good to know

- *Voy* (I'm going) is a form of *ir*; you will learn it later. Here it only shows *al*.
- *De* + person shows possession, where English has *'s*: *la casa de Ana* (Ana's house).

## Common mistakes

- ✗ *Coche es rojo.* → ✓ *El coche es rojo.* — a singular noun as the subject needs an article.
- ✗ *Voy a el cine.* → ✓ *Voy al cine.* — *a* + *el* always joins into *al*.
- ✗ *el libro de el profesor* → ✓ *el libro del profesor* — *de* + *el* joins into *del*.
- ✗ *El señor García, ¿cómo está?* (speaking to him) → ✓ *Señor García, ¿cómo está?* — no article when you speak directly to someone.
- ✗ *la agua* → ✓ *el agua* — a feminine noun with a stressed *a-* takes *el* in the singular.

## Practice

Write *el*, *la*, *los*, *las*, *un* or *una*.

1. Es ___ casa muy bonita. (a)
2. ___ niños son simpáticos. (the)
3. ___ ciudad es grande. (the)
4. Hay ___ problema. (a)

Write *al*, *del* or *de la*.

5. Voy ___ cine. (el cine)
6. Es el perro ___ vecino. (el vecino)
7. Es la casa ___ profesora. (la profesora)
8. Translate: Good afternoon, Mr. López.

## Answers

1. una
2. Los
3. La
4. un
5. al
6. del
7. de la
8. Buenas tardes, señor López.
