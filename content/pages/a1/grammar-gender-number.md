# Gender and number of nouns

In English a table is just "it". In Spanish every noun is **masculine** or **feminine**, even a table or an idea: *el libro* (the book) is masculine, *la mesa* (the table) is feminine. The gender has nothing to do with the thing itself, but it matters because the words around the noun change with it. Learn every new noun together with *el* or *la*.

## How it works

**Gender: most nouns show it in the ending.**

| Ending | Usually | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **-o** | masculine | *el libro*, *el vaso*, *el perro* |
| **-a** | feminine | *la mesa*, *la casa*, *la playa* |
| **-ción, -sión, -dad, -tad** | feminine | *la canción*, *la televisión*, *la ciudad*, *la libertad* |
| **-or, -aje** | masculine | *el color*, *el viaje* |
| **-e** or a consonant (other than -or, -aje) | either: learn it | *el coche*, *la noche*; *el hotel*, *la piel* |

Common exceptions: *el día*, *el mapa*, *el problema*, *el idioma*, *el sofá*; *la mano*, *la foto*, *la moto*, *la radio* (*el radio* for the device in Mexico and parts of Latin America), *la flor*.

**People:** many nouns change the ending (*el amigo*, *la amiga*; *el profesor*, *la profesora*). Nouns in *-ista* and *-e* often keep one form: *el / la estudiante*, *el / la periodista*.

**Number: making the plural.**

| Singular ends in | Add | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| a vowel | **-s** | *libro* → *libros*, *coche* → *coches* |
| a consonant | **-es** | *hotel* → *hoteles*, *ciudad* → *ciudades* |
| **-z** | z → **-ces** | *lápiz* → *lápices*, *vez* → *veces* |

- *el* → *los*, *la* → *las*: *los libros*, *las mesas*.
- When a word stressed on the last syllable ends in *-n* or *-s* and adds *-es*, the written accent disappears: *canción* → *canciones*, *inglés* → *ingleses*. A stressed final vowel keeps it: *sofá* → *sofás*.
- Words in unstressed *-s* do not change: *el lunes* → *los lunes*.
- A group of men and women takes the masculine plural: *los alumnos* can be all boys or boys and girls.

## When to use it

1. **Every time you learn a noun.** Learn *la ciudad*, not just *ciudad*. The article is your note of the gender.
2. **When you meet a new noun without an article.** Look at the ending and guess with the table; you will be right most of the time.
3. **Talking about more than one.** *Dos hoteles*, *tres canciones*, *veinte días*.
4. **Talking about mixed groups.** *Mis hermanos* (*mis* = my) can mean "my brothers" or "my brothers and sisters".

## Examples

| Spanish | English |
| --- | --- |
| el día y la noche | the day and the night |
| la mano | the hand |
| el problema | the problem |
| las ciudades | the cities |
| los lápices | the pencils |
| dos canciones | two songs |
| los estudiantes | the students (all men, or men and women) |
| las estudiantes | the students (all women) |

## Good to know

- Some nouns change meaning with the gender: *el capital* (money) and *la capital* (the capital city).
- Most countries and cities are used without an article: *Soy de Perú. Son de Madrid.*
- Spanish has no neuter "it" nouns: everything is *el* or *la*.

## Common mistakes

- ✗ *la problema* → ✓ *el problema* — *problema* ends in *-a* but is masculine.
- ✗ *el mano* → ✓ *la mano* — *mano* ends in *-o* but is feminine.
- ✗ *las ciudads* → ✓ *las ciudades* — after a consonant the plural adds *-es*.
- ✗ *los lápizs* → ✓ *los lápices* — a final *z* becomes *c* before *-es*.
