# Direct object pronouns and personal a

A direct object pronoun replaces the thing or person that receives the action: "I see **her**". Spanish chooses *lo* or *la* by the gender of the noun, so a book is *lo* and a house is *la*. The big difference from English is the position: the pronoun goes **before** the conjugated verb. *¿El libro? Lo compré ayer.* And when the object is a person, Spanish puts *a* in front of it: *Conozco a tu hermano.*

## How it works

**The pronouns**

| English | Spanish | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| me | **me** | *Ana me llama.* |
| you (a friend) | **te** | *Te veo mañana.* |
| him, it (masculine), you (formal, a man) | **lo** | *Lo compré ayer.* |
| her, it (feminine), you (formal, a woman) | **la** | *La conozco.* |
| us | **nos** | *Nos invitan.* |
| you all (Spain) | **os** | *Os espero.* |
| them (masculine or mixed), you all (ustedes) | **los** | *Los veo los domingos.* |
| them (feminine), you all (ustedes, only women) | **las** | *Las tengo aquí.* |

**Where it goes**

- Before the conjugated verb: *Lo veo. No lo veo. Lo he visto. La compré.*
- With an infinitive (*ir a*, *poder*, *querer*…) it goes before the first verb **or** is attached to the infinitive: *Lo voy a comprar* = *Voy a comprarlo.* Never in both places.
- *No* comes first: *No la conozco.*

**Personal a**

- A person as the object takes *a*: *Veo **a** María. Llamo **a** mis padres. ¿Conoces **a** Luis?*
- Things and places take no *a*: *Veo la tele. Conozco Lima.*

## When to use it

1. **To avoid repeating a noun.** *—¿Dónde está mi teléfono? —No lo sé. No lo veo.* (Where's my phone? I don't know. I don't see it.)
2. **Short answers.** *—¿Tienes las llaves? —Sí, las tengo.* (Yes, I have them.) English can say just "yes, I do"; Spanish needs the pronoun.
3. **To talk about people.** *—¿Conoces a Carmen? —Sí, la conozco del trabajo.* (Yes, I know her from work.)
4. **Lo for an idea.** *Lo sé.* (I know.) *No lo entiendo.* (I don't understand it.)

## Examples

| Spanish | English |
| --- | --- |
| ¿La bufanda? La compré en el mercado. | The scarf? I bought it at the market. |
| Te llamo esta tarde. | I'll call you this afternoon. |
| —¿Has visto a Pablo? —No, no lo he visto. | "Have you seen Pablo?" "No, I haven't seen him." |
| Mis abuelos viven lejos, pero los visito en verano. | My grandparents live far away, but I visit them in summer. |
| ¿Las fotos? Voy a enviarlas esta noche. | The photos? I'm going to send them tonight. |
| Busco a mi hermana. ¿La has visto? | I'm looking for my sister. Have you seen her? |
| Señora, la espero en la entrada. | Ma'am, I'll wait for you at the entrance. |

## Good to know

- **Leísmo (Spain).** In Spain many people say *le* instead of *lo* for a man: *Le vi ayer* (I saw him yesterday). The norm accepts it; this course uses *lo*: *Lo vi ayer.*
- The Spanish present often means "I'll…" in offers: *Te llamo esta tarde* (I'll call you this afternoon).
- After *a*, use the article as usual: *Veo **al** profesor* (a + el = al).

## Common mistakes

- ✗ *Veo lo.* → ✓ *Lo veo.* — the pronoun goes before the conjugated verb.
- ✗ *Veo María.* → ✓ *Veo a María.* — a person as the object needs *a*.
- ✗ *—¿Tienes el libro? —Sí, tengo.* → ✓ *Sí, lo tengo.* — Spanish needs the pronoun.
- ✗ *Lo voy a comprarlo.* → ✓ *Lo voy a comprar.* / *Voy a comprarlo.* — the pronoun goes in one place only.

3. No conozco **a tu novia**.

## Answers

1. Lo compro todos los días.
2. Los llamé ayer.
3. No la conozco.
4. Las hemos visto.
5. a (Visité a mis tíos en Bogotá.)
6. ¿La camisa? La compré ayer.
