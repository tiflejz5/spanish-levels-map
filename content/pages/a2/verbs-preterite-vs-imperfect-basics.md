# Indefinido vs imperfecto: first contrast

Spanish has two simple past tenses, and English has one. "It rained" can be *llovió* or *llovía*, and the choice depends on what you want to say. Think of a film: the imperfect (*llovía*) describes the scene in the background; the preterite (*salí*) is the action that moves the story on. *Llovía cuando salí de casa.* (It was raining when I left home.)

## How it works

| Imperfect (*hablaba*) | Preterite (*hablé*) |
| --- | --- |
| the scene: what things were like | the events: what happened |
| an action in progress, without an end | a finished action, often with a start or an end |
| a habit: what used to happen | one time, or a counted number of times |
| *siempre, todos los días, normalmente, antes* | *ayer, una vez, el lunes, de repente* (suddenly), *tres veces* |

**The basic pattern:** an action in progress (imperfect) is interrupted by a new event (preterite).

*Cenábamos **cuando** llamó Ana.* (We were having dinner when Ana called.)

- The imperfect can often be translated "was / were + -ing": *Leía* = I was reading.
- The same verb changes meaning with the tense: *Fue un día largo* (the day as a whole, now over) / *Era un día de sol* (description of the setting).

## When to use it

1. **Background + event.** *Hacía sol cuando llegamos a la playa.* (It was sunny when we got to the beach.)
2. **Interrupted action.** *Dormía cuando sonó el teléfono.* (I was sleeping when the phone rang.)
3. **Habit vs one time.** *Siempre comíamos paella los domingos, pero ese domingo comimos pizza.* (We always had paella on Sundays, but that Sunday we had pizza.)
4. **Description vs action in a story.** *La casa era vieja y oscura. Abrí la puerta y entré.* (The house was old and dark. I opened the door and went in.)
5. **A closed period counts as an event.** *Viví en Roma tres años.* (I lived in Rome for three years.) But: *Cuando vivía en Roma, iba mucho al cine.* (When I lived in Rome, I often went to the movies.)

## Examples

| Spanish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Llovía cuando salí de casa. | It was raining when I left home. |
| Mientras yo cocinaba, Pablo puso la mesa. | While I was cooking, Pablo set the table. |
| Eran las once cuando terminó la película. | It was eleven when the movie ended. |
| Antes iba al gimnasio, pero el año pasado empecé a correr. | I used to go to the gym, but last year I started running. |
| Estaba muy cansada, así que me acosté temprano. | I was very tired, so I went to bed early. |
| Conocí a Laura cuando trabajaba en Bogotá. | I met Laura when I was working in Bogotá. |
| De repente, empezó a llover. | Suddenly, it started to rain. |

## Good to know

- *Mientras* (while) is usually followed by the imperfect: *Mientras esperaba el autobús, leí las noticias.*
- *Cuando* can go with both. Ask yourself: is it a background (imperfect) or an event (preterite)? *Cuando era joven…* / *Cuando llegué…*

## Common mistakes

- ✗ *Cuando llegué a casa, mi madre cocinó.* → ✓ *Cuando llegué a casa, mi madre cocinaba.* — she was in the middle of it: imperfect.
- ✗ *De niño fui a la playa todos los veranos.* → ✓ *De niño iba a la playa todos los veranos.* — a repeated habit takes the imperfect.
- ✗ *Ayer veía una película muy buena.* → ✓ *Ayer vi una película muy buena.* — one finished action yesterday: preterite.
- ✗ *Estaba en Roma tres días.* → ✓ *Estuve en Roma tres días.* — a closed period is an event: preterite.

## Practice

Choose the imperfect or the preterite.

1. Ayer ___ (llover) todo el día.
2. Cuando era pequeña, ___ (tener) un perro.
3. ___ (ducharse, yo) cuando llegó el cartero.
4. El sábado pasado ___ (ir, nosotros) al cine.
5. Antes Carlos ___ (trabajar) los fines de semana.
6. La habitación ___ (ser) pequeña, pero ___ (tener) mucha luz.

Translate into Spanish.

7. I was reading when you called. (to a friend)

## Answers

1. llovió
2. tenía
3. Me duchaba
4. fuimos
5. trabajaba
6. era, tenía
7. Leía cuando llamaste. / Leía cuando me llamaste.
