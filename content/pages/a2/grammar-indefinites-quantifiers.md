# Indefinites and quantifiers

Indefinites talk about things and people without naming them: *algo* (something), *alguien* (somebody). Each one has a negative partner: *nada, nadie, ningún*. You already know from A1 that Spanish uses a **double negative**: *No veo a nadie* (I don't see anybody, word for word "I don't see nobody"). Quantifiers say how much: *mucho, poco, bastante, demasiado*. Unlike English "much" and "many", they agree with the noun: *mucho tiempo, muchas personas*.

## How it works

**Indefinites and their negative partners**

| Positive | Negative | Used for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **algo** (something, anything) | **nada** (nothing, not anything) | things, no noun after it |
| **alguien** (somebody, anybody) | **nadie** (nobody, not anybody) | people, no noun after it |
| **algún / alguna / algunos / algunas**; alone: **alguno** (some, any) | **ningún / ninguna**; alone: **ninguno** (no, not any) | before a noun, or alone |

- *Alguno* and *ninguno* lose the **-o** before a masculine noun: *algún libro, ningún problema.* Alone they keep it: *¿Tienes algún libro? —No, no tengo ninguno.*
- *Ningún / ninguna* are almost always singular.
- A negative word **after** the verb needs *no* before the verb: *No hay nada. No viene nadie.* **Before** the verb it works alone: *Nadie viene.*
- *Alguien* and *nadie* as objects take the personal *a*: *¿Conoces a alguien aquí? No conozco a nadie.*

**otro** (another, other)

- *Otro, otra, otros, otras* agree with the noun and never take *un / una*: *otro café* (another coffee), *otra vez* (again).

**Quantifiers**

| | with a noun (agrees) | with a verb or adjective (never changes) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| a lot, very | **mucho, mucha, muchos, muchas** | *mucho* (verb) / **muy** (adjective) |
| a little, few | **poco, poca, pocos, pocas** | *poco* |
| enough, quite | **bastante, bastantes** | *bastante* |
| too much, too many | **demasiado, demasiada, demasiados, demasiadas** | *demasiado* |

- With a noun they agree: *muchas horas, poca gente, bastantes sillas, demasiada sal.*
- With a verb they never change: *Trabaja mucho. Come poco. Duermo bastante.*
- Before an adjective or adverb, "very" is **muy**, not *mucho*: *Es muy caro. Vive muy lejos.*

## When to use it

1. **Asking about something or someone unknown.** *¿Quieres algo? ¿Ha llamado alguien?* (Do you want anything? Has anybody called?)
2. **Answering no.** *—¿Necesitas algo? —No, nada, gracias.* (No, nothing, thanks.)
3. **Asking if there is any.** *¿Hay algún banco por aquí?* (Is there a bank around here?)
4. **Saying how much.** *Hoy tengo poco tiempo. Hay demasiados coches en el centro.* (I have little time today. There are too many cars downtown.)

## Examples

| Spanish | English |
| --- | --- |
| ¿Hay alguien en casa? | Is anybody home? |
| No he comprado nada para la cena. | I haven't bought anything for dinner. |
| ¿Tienes alguna pregunta? | Do you have any questions? |
| No hay ningún problema. | There's no problem at all. |
| En la fiesta había mucha gente. | There were a lot of people at the party. |
| Mis hijos ven demasiada televisión. | My children watch too much TV. |

## Good to know

- *La gente* (people) is singular in Spanish: *mucha gente, poca gente*, and the verb is singular: *La gente es muy simpática.*

## Common mistakes

- ✗ *Quiero un otro café.* → ✓ *Quiero otro café.* — *otro* never takes *un*.
- ✗ *¿Tienes alguno libro?* → ✓ *¿Tienes algún libro?* — before a masculine noun, *alguno* loses the *-o*.
- ✗ *No conozco a alguien aquí.* → ✓ *No conozco a nadie aquí.* — after *no*, use the negative word *nadie*.
- ✗ *Es mucho caro.* → ✓ *Es muy caro.* — before an adjective, "very" is *muy*.
- ✗ *Hay muchos gente.* → ✓ *Hay mucha gente.* — *gente* is feminine and singular.
