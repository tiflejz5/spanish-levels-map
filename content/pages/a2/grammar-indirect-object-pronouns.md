# Indirect object pronouns

An indirect object is the person who **receives** something or for whom something is done: "I give **her** the book", "I write **to them**". You already know these pronouns from *gustar*: *me, te, le, nos, os, les*. They go where *lo* and *la* go: before the conjugated verb. Unlike English, Spanish often keeps the pronoun even when the person is named: *Le doy el libro a Ana*.

## How it works

**The pronouns**

| English | Spanish | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| (to) me | **me** | *¿Me dices la hora?* |
| (to) you (a friend) | **te** | *Te escribo luego.* |
| (to) him, her, you (formal) | **le** | *Le doy el libro.* |
| (to) us | **nos** | *Nos explicaron el plan.* |
| (to) you all (Spain) | **os** | *Os mando las fotos.* |
| (to) them, you all (ustedes) | **les** | *Les escribo cada semana.* |

- Only *le* and *les* differ from the direct object pronouns, and they do not change for gender: *le* = to him **or** to her.

**Where it goes**: exactly like *lo* and *la*.

- Before the conjugated verb: *Le escribí ayer. No le he dicho nada.*
- With an infinitive, before the first verb or attached: *Te voy a dar un regalo* = *Voy a darte un regalo.*

**Saying the person twice.** With *le* and *les*, add *a* + the person to make clear who it is: *Le doy el libro **a Ana**. Les escribo **a mis abuelos**.* This is normal Spanish, not a repetition to avoid.

## When to use it

1. **Giving and sending:** *dar, regalar, mandar, enviar, prestar.* *Le regalé un libro a mi padre.* (I gave my father a book.)
2. **Telling and asking:** *decir, contar, explicar, preguntar, escribir.* *¿Me explicas el ejercicio?* (Can you explain the exercise to me?)
3. **Small requests to a friend:** *¿Me pasas la sal?* (Can you pass me the salt?)

## Examples

| Spanish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Le he dicho la verdad a Lucía. | I've told Lucía the truth. |
| ¿Me prestas tu bicicleta? | Can you lend me your bike? |
| Mis padres nos mandaron un paquete. | My parents sent us a package. |
| Voy a escribirles un correo a mis profesores. | I'm going to write an email to my teachers. |
| El médico le explicó todo a mi madre. | The doctor explained everything to my mother. |
| ¿Qué le vas a regalar a tu hermano? | What are you going to give your brother? |

## Good to know

- **Two pronouns together.** The indirect one comes first: *¿Me lo repites?* (Can you repeat it for me?) With *le / les* + *lo / la*, *le* changes to *se*: *Se lo doy.* This is a later topic; for now, learn *¿Me lo repites?* as a phrase.
- *Llamar* takes a direct object: *La llamo* (I call her). *Preguntar* takes an indirect one: *Le pregunto* (I ask her).

## Common mistakes

- ✗ *Doy le el libro.* → ✓ *Le doy el libro.* — the pronoun goes before the conjugated verb.
- ✗ *Lo doy un regalo a mi hermano.* → ✓ *Le doy un regalo a mi hermano.* — "to him" is *le*; *lo* means "him" or "it".
- ✗ *Escribí a ella un correo.* → ✓ *Le escribí un correo.* — with *a ella* or *a él* you must add *le*.
- ✗ *Le pregunté una pregunta.* → ✓ *Le hice una pregunta.* — "to ask a question" is *hacer una pregunta*.

## Practice

Complete with *me, te, le, nos* or *les*.

1. Mañana es el cumpleaños de mi madre. Voy a regalar___ flores.
2. ¿___ dices tu número de teléfono? (to me)
3. Mis abuelos viven en Chile y ___ escribo cada mes.
4. El profesor ___ explicó la regla. (to us)

Put the words in order.

5. libro / le / un / regalé / a Marta

Translate into Spanish.

6. Can you lend me your pen? (to a friend)
7. I told her the truth.

## Answers

1. regalarle (Le voy a regalar flores.)
2. Me
3. les
4. nos
5. Le regalé un libro a Marta. / A Marta le regalé un libro.
6. ¿Me prestas tu bolígrafo? / ¿Me prestas tu boli? (Spain) / ¿Me prestas tu pluma? (Mexico)
7. Le dije la verdad. / Le dije la verdad a ella.
