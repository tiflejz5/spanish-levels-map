## A2 in a nutshell

**What you can do at this level**

You can tell what you did last weekend or on vacation, describe how things used to be when you were a child, and say what you have or haven't done yet. You can describe people, places and things, compare them, and talk about what is happening now and what will happen. You can get around: buy tickets, book a room or a table, ask the way and follow directions, see a doctor or a pharmacist, make plans with friends and report a simple problem politely.

**Key ideas**

- **Two simple past tenses.** The preterite tells what happened (*Ayer fui al cine*); the imperfect describes the scene and habits (*De niño iba mucho al cine*). Together: *Llovía cuando salí de casa.*
- **The present perfect looks at the past from now:** experiences and things not done yet (*¿Has estado alguna vez en México? Todavía no he comido*). In Spain it is also normal for today.
- **Object pronouns go before the verb:** *Lo compré ayer. Le doy el libro a Ana.* With an infinitive or gerund they may also be attached: *Voy a comprarlo.*
- **por and para both mean "for":** *para* looks ahead to a goal (*para ti, para viajar*), *por* looks at the cause or the way (*gracias por todo, por teléfono*).
- **Ordinary verbs + infinitive** do the work of "must", "can" and "will": *tengo que, hay que, puedo, sé nadar*; and the future adds endings to the infinitive: *llamaré, comeré* (a few verbs have short stems: *tendré, haré*).

**The most common trap**

Choosing the past tense by the English verb. "I was" can be *estuve*, *fui*, *estaba* or *era*; ask whether it is an event (preterite) or a background or habit (imperfect), and use the preterite with a finished time like *ayer* or *el año pasado*, never the perfect.

**How long it takes**

- Going from A1 to A2 usually takes **about 100–150 more hours** of guided study plus practice on your own.
- With **3–4 hours a week**, that is roughly **7–10 months**. Telling short stories about your own day out loud is the fastest way to make the past tenses automatic.

*These are typical ranges for adult learners; your pace depends on how much time you spend with the language.*

**What next**

You are ready for B1 when you can tell a short story about a trip or your childhood in two or three minutes, mixing the preterite and the imperfect, and deal with a hotel, a doctor or a ticket office without switching to English. At B1 you will meet the subjunctive (*quiero que vengas*), the conditional (*me gustaría*) and the pluperfect (*ya había salido*), and learn to give opinions and argue a point.
