## A1 in a nutshell

**What you can do at this level**

You can introduce yourself and others, say where you are from and what you do, and ask the same questions back. You can talk about your family, your home and your town, your day and your plans, and you can order in a café, buy food and ask what things cost. You understand people who speak slowly and clearly, and you know how to ask them to repeat.

**Key ideas**

- **Two verbs for "to be".** *Ser* says what something is (*Soy profesora*, *Es de Chile*); *estar* says where it is and how it is now (*Estoy en casa*, *Estoy cansada*).
- **Endings show the person.** *Hablo, hablas, habla…*: the pronoun is usually left out.
- **Every noun is masculine or feminine,** and articles and adjectives agree with it: *la casa blanca*, *los coches rojos*.
- **Gustar works backwards:** the thing you like is the subject. *Me gusta el café. Me gustan los gatos.*
- **Ir a + infinitive** is your first future: *Voy a estudiar.*

**The most common trap**

Translating "to be" word for word. Age, hunger and cold take *tener* (*Tengo 30 años*, *tengo hambre*), location takes *estar*, and a job takes *ser* without *un / una* (*Es médico*).

**How long it takes**

- Reaching A1 from zero usually takes **about 80–100 hours** of guided study plus practice on your own.
- With **3–4 hours a week**, that is roughly **5–7 months**. Speaking a little every day helps more than one long session a week.

*These are typical ranges for adult learners; your pace depends on how much time you spend with the language.*

**What next**

You are ready for A2 when you can talk for two or three minutes about yourself, your family and your day, and ask someone a few questions about theirs. At A2 you will learn to talk about the past (*ayer comí*, *de niño vivía*) and to describe things in more detail.
